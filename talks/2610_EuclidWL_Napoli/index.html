The SPHEREx satellite

2.6 m tall · 3.2 m across · 502 kg · 20 cm effective aperture  ·  NASA/JPL-Caltech, PIA26537: SPHEREx after environmental testing, BAE Systems, Boulder, November 2024.

The observatory a few months before launch. Right to left: the big white cone is the outer photon shield, and there are three of them nested. They are baffles, not a JWST-style sunshade: they keep sunlight and Earthshine off the telescope while it looks away from the Sun. Behind them the small drum is the telescope, 20 cm effective aperture. The red slatted panel and gold blanketing are the V-groove radiators that cool it passively; the flat black panel is the spacecraft bus. For scale (JPL press kit): 2.6 m tall, 3.2 m wide, 502 kg. A Medium-Class Explorer, not a flagship; it launched on a Falcon 9, sharing the ride with PUNCH. The 3.2 m bar is the mouth of the outer photon shield, the widest part. The 1 m bar is exact only at roughly that depth: the left end of the spacecraft is further from the camera. Slide and overlay from the CosmoStat journal-club deck.

Animation: NASA/JPL-Caltech (NASA SVS), "Novel Survey Strategy". Numbers: Bock et al. 2025 (arXiv:2511.02985).

The observing geometry: the observatory works near the great circle 90° from the Sun, and that circle sweeps round the sky as the Earth goes round the Sun, so the whole sky is covered every six months. The inset is the allowed pointing zone. Sun-synchronous polar orbit, inclination 97.95°, Sun kept more than 93.5° off axis. The boresight is tipped 8° toward the solar panels, which is what lets it reach the ecliptic poles. Cooling: three conical photon shields and a three-stage V-groove radiator hold the optics near 55 K. No cryogen and no mechanical cooler, so no consumable sets the mission lifetime. The poles are crossed on nearly every orbit, so the deep fields are a gift of geometry, not an allocation. That is also why SPHEREx depth in our footprint depends on ecliptic latitude (the SPHEREx depth slide, after the release timeline).

Helium in the upper atmosphere glows at 1.083 µm

The physics

Helium atoms high in the atmosphere sit in a long-lived excited state, and scatter sunlight at exactly 1.083 µm. Hubble's infrared camera sees the same glow.

What it did

Left in the spectrum, the line is read as Hα at z ≈ 0.67: a pile of false redshifts at 0.6–0.68, and a cliff at 0.68.

A galaxy at DESI z = 0.12, fitted as published at z = 0.67;
without the He I channels, z = 0.11.

understand this better (just a note for calum)

Metastable 2³S helium in the upper atmosphere resonantly scatters sunlight in the 2³S–2³P transition at 1.083 µm; Hubble's WFC3 sees it as a time-varying background (Brammer et al. 2014).

In one SPHEREx D1 frame (2025-10-20) the sky is 0.40 MJy/sr at 1.040 µm, 2.43 on the line and 0.47 at 1.106 µm. In the co-added spectra a residual on SPHEREx channels 14–15 is read as Hα at z ≈ 0.67, which made the alias pile at 0.6–0.68 and the cliff at 0.68 in the catalogue (issue #11).

The fix so far is a fit-level mask of the He I channels; the next step is SPHEREx's published He I background template (#49), and the adopted tractorjax-spherex background with 288 wavelength bins catches the line's edge across a whole frame (DECISIONS, EXT-20).

Bright stars pull redshifts into rings around them

How it showed up

Redshift shells that anti-correlate. In linear theory two shells can only correlate positively.

The cause

The halo of a bright star leaks into the local background around each galaxy: z slides below 0.1 near the star, and into 0.5–0.7 in a ring outside it.

The fix

Measure the background as a clipped median over all usable pixels, not a thin annulus. Inside the halo radius, outliers fall from 22.8% to 3.8%.

Galaxies round three bright stars: fitted at z < 0.1 (blue) or 0.5 < z < 0.7 (orange). Left, the annulus background as published; right, the clipped median.

The same sky in SPHEREx D3 (1.6–2.4 µm), median of 127 frames: the halos of the two brightest stars are visible directly.

Found because redshift shells anti-correlated, which linear theory forbids, and because the systematics weights would not converge (CATALOGUE_REPORT.md §8.6, issue #2).

Around three stars of Ks 0.2, 0.4 and 1.9: before, 1,026 galaxies fitted at z < 0.1 and 1,411 at 0.5–0.7; after the F4 background (clipped median over all usable pixels), 886 and 546. Against DESI, inside the halo radius: 22.8% outliers on 3,503 galaxies before, 3.8% on 3,647 after (results/figures/talk/halo_zz.png).

The release also carries the veto radius 0.1° × 10^(0.2(5.5 − Ks)), capped at 3°, round 188,347 2MASS stars (column bs_ratio). In the final background, tractorjax-spherex's map is adopted with F4 kept inside bright-star halo discs (DECISIONS, EXT-20).

Galaxy auto/cross-correlations

How the weights are made

Eight sky maps: dust, star density, SPHEREx channel count, ecliptic latitude, zodi, background, visits, Euclid VIS depth; five are regressed.

In each shell, galaxy density is binned against each map. A trend counts only if it is 2× larger than in 400 lognormal mocks, which cluster but are clean.

Remove the worst trend with a linear fit, repeat until all pass, and weight each galaxy by 1/fit in its own shell.

What it shows

Neighbouring shells correlate (r = 0.15–0.33, the photo-z overlap); shells two or more apart are near zero.

SPHEREx depth

Euclid depth is uniform (tiling at constant exposure), varying weakly with Galactic latitude. SPHEREx revisits the ecliptic poles constantly, so depth is a function of ecliptic latitude: 252 obs at 30°, 325 at 70°, 1,277 beyond 80°. The footprint stops at 87.8° because the poles are Euclid deep fields. The two are essentially uncorrelated (report Fig. 6), so they act as independent axes of the selection function. Median co-added S/N over all targets in the v2 catalogue 11.0 (16-84%: 4.7-34.8); median 333 observations per target. Median E(B-V) 0.022 (CSFD, over targets). Direct side: the n(z) of each z_phot bin from the matched spec-z, weighted by catalogue/truth counts in (I_E, z_est) cells, north lobe (make_figures.py, direct_nz). The error on the mean is a jackknife over HEALPix nside-4 patches (~200 deg² each). It does NOT include the representativeness error: the reweighting assumes the truth sample is fair within each (I_E, z_est) cell, which DESI targeting may violate. So these are lower limits on the direct error, and the honest statement is "comparable or better" in bin 1. Clustering side: Cawthon et al. 2022 (DES Y3, clustering redshifts against BOSS/eBOSS for MagLim and redMaGiC; arXiv:2012.12826): "Our typical uncertainties on the mean redshift ranged from 0.003 to 0.008, while our uncertainties on the width ranged from 4 to 9%." That is over ~4,000 deg² with BOSS/eBOSS as reference; a clustering-z for this catalogue against DESI over ~450 deg² (north) would have its own error, not forecast here. Effective numbers fall fast with z: 3,527 in 0.20-0.40, 788 in 0.40-0.55, 679 in 0.55-0.70. Above 0.55 the spec-z overlap is what limits the direct method, and that is where a clustering-z (needing no spec-z of these galaxies) would add something. One run fits two hypotheses

Galaxy fit

  • 77 SPHEREx channels only, He I channels masked
  • fluxes corrected to total for galaxy size
  • 12 templates, non-negative, z = 0.001–2
  • → zest, P(z), χ²gal

Stellar fit

  • same photometry, same weights
  • PHOENIX/Sonora grid, no redshift
  • best single template → χ²★, Teff, log g
  • is_star if χ²★ − χ²gal < 0
Stellar templates are not redshifted, so they are a second grid alongside the galaxy fit, not extra galaxy templates: eazy's fit_phoenix_stars fits every PHOENIX/Sonora template to the same fluxes with the same weights, and delta_chi2_star = best stellar χ² − galaxy χ². is_star = delta_chi2_star < 0 (STAR_DCHI2_MAX in src/redshift/estimate_redshift.py). Carried: chi2_star, delta_chi2_star, star_teff, star_logg. Calibration on Euclid (src/calibrate_star_flag.py): 257,637 Gaia astrometric stars vs 515,519 confirmed galaxies over the pilot; delta_chi2_star median −3,559 for stars vs +377 for galaxies at 18 ≤ I_E < 19; at threshold 0: 0.91 completeness, 0.07 galaxy loss there; Youden optimum +4.2. The scale-free ratio would separate better (AUC 0.93 vs 0.90) but the number needs no change. It goes blind fainter than I_E 20.5, where the spectra no longer distinguish a star from a galaxy. The Gaia calibration above was measured on the September (joint-fit) catalogue and has not been repeated on v2. v2 caveat: the flag is only set on objects whose galaxy fit converged; 5.75 M objects whose galaxy fit lands on the grid floor carry no is_star although 71% of them are point-like (homogeneity slide). v2 has no zeropoints in the fit: no optical band enters it.

The previous slide asked it pixel by pixel; now scale by scale

The idea in one sentence: if the galaxy map contains a copy of the template map, the two share structure, and the cross-spectrum C^gt says at which angular scales they share it. Left: galaxy overdensity of the clustering sample at 0.3 ≤ z_est < 0.4 (0.5° pixels). Right: the template, here the number of SPHEREx observations per target. By eye the galaxy map is mostly cosmic structure plus noise; the question is whether any of the fan-shaped depth pattern on the right leaks into it. Relation to slide 21: that slide bins pixels by template value and plots the mean galaxy density in each bin (a 1-D relation, all scales mixed together). This asks the same question per angular scale ℓ (ℓ ≈ 180°/θ: ℓ = 20 is ~9°, ℓ = 200 is ~1°). Both use the same galaxies; slide 21 is from the pipeline with ISD weights, these slides are new, local and unweighted. Why scale matters: contamination that lives on degree scales biases the BAO and large-scale bias; contamination at small scales matters less for us.

Definitions

  • galaxies: \( \delta_g = \dfrac{n}{\bar n\, f} - 1 \) per pixel (f = covered fraction)

n: galaxies in the pixel; n̄: mean over the mask; f: fraction of the pixel covered by the footprint (from target occupancy at 3.4′), so edge pixels are not read as underdense. Mask: pixels ≥ 80% covered. Templates are the per-pixel mean of per-object catalogue columns, then standardised, so C^tt and C^gt are comparable across templates. log n_obs because depth goes as the log of the exposure count. a_ℓm are the spherical-harmonic coefficients of the map (healpy.anafast); dividing by f_sky is the simplest partial-sky correction (no mode-coupling matrix; NaMaster on candide does this properly). Shot noise: 1/n̄ (per steradian) is subtracted from C^gg only; the star map has ~300 stars per pixel, so its shot noise is negligible. Linear contamination model: if the observed field is the true field plus ε times the template, then C^gt = ε C^tt and C^gg picks up ε² C^tt, which is r² of the total. r = 0.3 → 9% of the power. Error: for no true correlation, r_ℓ scatters with σ = 1/√N_modes, N_modes = f_sky Σ(2ℓ+1) over the bin. Bins are treated as independent (approximately true for these wide bins).

How a flux is measured: the PSF and the Tractor fit

The PSF comes with the frame

Every frame carries its own PSF: 121 zones (11 × 11 across the detector), each 10× oversampled, built by the mission from >1,000 stars per zone. We use the zone nearest the source.

Placed before it is binned

At 6.2″ the pixels undersample the PSF, so where a source sits inside a pixel changes how its light splits between pixels. We shift the 10× PSF to the source's sub-pixel position, then sum it into 9 × 9 native pixels. Binning first and shifting after loses 6–9% of the flux, worst on the sharp blue detectors.

Point source, fixed position

The Euclid (or Legacy) position goes through the frame's WCS. The galaxies are a fraction of a pixel (IE 19–20: ~0.75″), so a point source is the model.

One free parameter: the flux

Background already subtracted, sky fixed at zero, position and PSF frozen. What is left is a weighted linear fit of the PSF P to the 33 × 33 px stamp d:

\[ \hat F = \frac{\sum_i w_i P_i d_i}{\sum_i w_i P_i^2}, \qquad \sigma_F = \Big(\sum_i w_i P_i^2\Big)^{-1/2} \]

Tractor does this solve (a full fit and the closed form agree to float precision). Flagged pixels get w = 0. Weights: 1/variance in the Euclid catalogue; in the all-sky final run a clipped variance, because the shipped variance uses each pixel's own noise and biases every flux low.

Then many times over

One flux and error per target per frame, hundreds per target, each with its own wavelength from where it fell on the filter; co-added onto 77 channels.

Checked against IRSA's own spectrophotometry tool: flux scale 1.036, no trend with wavelength. What the model cannot do: large galaxies lose light outside the PSF, and a neighbour inside the PSF adds its own (no neighbours are fitted).

Code: spherex-allsky src/spectra/extract_spectra.py (psf_zone, native_psf, measure_target, measure_stamp); the Euclid extractor (euclid-spherex src/spectra/extract_spectra.py) has the same psf_zone, native_psf and Tractor call. PSF: the L2B frame's PSF extension is a cube of 121 zones on an 11 × 11 grid over the detector, each 101 × 101 at 10× oversampling (centre index 50); the mission builds it from more than 1,000 stars per zone and band (SPHEREx pipeline paper, as summarised in spherex-allsky docs/SPHEREX_ONLINE_2026-10-02.md). psf_zone picks the zone whose centre (XCTR_n, YCTR_n) is nearest the source's detector position. The PSF is not interpolated between zones; tractorjax blends neighbouring zones bilinearly, effect unquantified. Zone off-by-two (issue #63): the zone centres are 1-based; the Euclid extractor and allsky_v1 compared them with sx − 1 instead of sx + 1, which picks the neighbouring zone for ~2% of positions; on 120 real observations one flux changed. Fixed in the all-sky final run (--psf-zone-onebased); not re-run on Euclid. Sub-pixel placement (native_psf): the oversampled zone PSF is shifted by the source's sub-pixel offset (dx, dy) × 10 with a cubic spline, then 10 × 10 blocks are summed into a 9 × 9 native-pixel stamp, normalised to unit sum. The source is then pinned at the integer pixel so Tractor applies no further shift. The comment in the code: block-reducing first and letting Tractor Lanczos-shift the native stamp "smears and loses ~6-9% of the flux (worst for the sharp blue detectors)". This is the "PSF undersampling fix" settled in the UNIONS predecessor, and it reproduces the official IRSA/Level-3 fluxes. The code refuses any PSF header whose oversampling is not 10 (EXT-24), rather than mis-sample. Fit: Tractor Image with data = stamp minus background, invvar = weights, PixelizedPSF(native PSF), ConstantSky(0), LinearPhotoCal(1); PointSource at the integer pixel with position frozen, image parameters frozen; tr.optimize_forced_photometry(variance=True). With only the flux free this is linear least squares: F = Σ w P d / Σ w P², error from the inverse variance Σ w P². DECISIONS 2026-10-07 (EXT-67): a direct weighted solve f = Σ(wPd)/Σ(wP²) equals the full Tractor fit to float precision, which is how the second (F4c) flux is computed cheaply. If nothing constrains the fit (every pixel under the PSF masked, e.g. the D3/D4 band edge at 2.40-2.44 µm) the observation is reported as failed rather than given the seed flux. Weights (issue #47): the L2 VARIANCE's Poisson term is computed from each pixel's own measured signal, so 1/VARIANCE down-weights pixels that fluctuated high and every flux is biased low by a detector-dependent constant (blank sky −1 to −2 µJy on D1-D4, −9 on D5, −17 on D6), the same on every visit, so co-adding does not remove it. The all-sky final run uses "clip3": VARIANCE − g·clip(d, ±3σ), which removes the pixel's own signal and noise for faint sources and leaves bright ones essentially unchanged. The Euclid catalogue uses plain 1/VARIANCE. Also recorded per observation: an aperture flux inside r = 3 px (a diagnostic of how much light a point-source model misses on extended galaxies), the wavelength and bandwidth at the source pixel from the frame's wavelength WCS, flags, and fit-quality statistics. Validation against IRSA (Euclid report, pilot): after the PSF correction the two agree to an inverse-variance-weighted flux scale of 1.036 with a median pull of +0.075, and no residual trend with wavelength; a few-per-cent grey offset is absorbed by the zero points and the template normalisation. Limits: extended galaxies lose flux to the point-source model (bright extended Euclid galaxies 30-40%; in the all-sky run galaxies above 3″ lose a median 29%, and the loss is not grey above 4 µm). No neighbours are modelled, so a neighbour inside the 6″ PSF adds its light (some catastrophic failures show this); joint neighbour fitting was tested in the tractorjax audit and not adopted, for speed.
Backup · sky background

Every SPHEREx pixel is mostly foreground

What is left after the shipped zodiacal model is subtracted, as a fraction of that model, per channel (all-sky QR2 frames, |b| > 30). The spike at 1.083 µm is He I airglow.

Zodiacal light comes modelled

Every L2B frame carries a ZODI plane (Kelsall et al. 1998 model, Tsumura et al. 2013 scaling). It is not subtracted from the image: that is left to the user.

The model is not the whole sky

At |b| > 30, sky − ZODI is still 20–40% of ZODI: 60–300 µJy on a point-source flux, as bright as a faint target. Plus He I airglow at 1.083 µm, and bright-star halos.

So we subtract it before the fit

Tractor is given a sky fixed at zero. The only free parameter is the source's flux, at the fixed Euclid position with the frame's PSF.

Every SPHEREx L2B frame has IMAGE, VARIANCE, FLAGS, ZODI and PSF extensions plus a wavelength WCS. The ZODI plane is the zodiacal-light model (Kelsall 1998 + Tsumura 2013 scaling); the mission paper (arXiv:2511.15823) says it is not subtracted from IMAGE. SPHEREx's own Level-3 plan is a zodi spectral fit then photutils Background2D; we do not use L3. Residual after ZODI (spherex-allsky results/zodi75/README.md, figure zodi75/levels.png): sky − ZODI is 0.032-0.096 MJy/sr, 21-40% of ZODI at |b| > 30, i.e. roughly 60-300 µJy on a PSF flux. So a background step is not optional. Design shared by every pipeline here: the background is subtracted from the stamp BEFORE the Tractor fit (ConstantSky(0) and frozen image parameters, euclid-spherex src/spectra/extract_spectra.py:279), and the fit solves only for the flux at the fixed Euclid position. A free sky per stamp was rejected in the all-sky work: without neighbours modelled it biases single-source fluxes 6-9% low (tractorjax audit, 2026-10-04).

tractorjax-spherex, and what we took from it

tractorjax-spherex (Bahk et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.19875; code hbahk/tractorjax-spherex 0.3.3 at 9266a19 with spherex-retrieval 0.4.0). The stages are from their code as read in our audit (spherex-allsky results/tractorjax/README.md, issue #65; results/bkgmap/README.md, #64; docs/SPHEREX_ONLINE_2026-10-02.md). The paper itself validates on simulated images (SPHEREx Sky Simulator, 21 × 21 px cutouts, a pre-launch PSF, zodi-only background) and does not describe the background map, the tiles, the free sky or JAX: those come from the code. The clones are on nibi, not on this laptop. Inputs: IRSA cutouts per field via spherex-retrieval, not whole frames (code defaults attach the R7 ePSF and the R7 l3 gain; their paper configuration uses the QR2 PSF cube, gain off). Source list: every Legacy source with m_z < 21 plus the targets, with fixed Legacy Sérsic shapes convolved with the PSF. Background per cutout: ZODI + a PCHIP profile through 48 quantile bins of per-pixel wavelength (≥ 20 px per bin) + photutils Background2D, box 10, filter 3, otherwise defaults (exclude_percentile 10); mask = their mask bits (including OUTLIER) | MP_SOURCE | bad variance. Fit: 15 px tiles with a 3 px halo, all sources in a tile fitted jointly with a free sky constant per tile; PSF zone kernels blended bilinearly at the tile centre; 1/variance weights; tractor-jax (JAX, GPU or CPU) with their eigfloor solver, or a CPU linear-solver backend. Output: a flux and error per source per visit, λ/Δλ, a χ² quality flag (> 10× the source median). Spectral product: inverse-variance binning onto channels, flagged visits dropped, no clip; the paper averages onto 102 channels and runs eazy-py. What we did (DECISIONS 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-07; results/tractorjax, results/bkgmap): TAKEN, their background map. Swapped alone into our fit on 1,038 DESI galaxies it gave Δσ_NMAD −1.70 × 10⁻³ and Δη −0.96%. CHANGED: 288 wavelength bins over whole frames instead of 48 per cutout (with 48 bins over a whole frame the He I edge leaked, which carried 85% of the gap; the fixed port recovers 81% of their gain); the halo-aware mask inside Ks < 8 2MASS discs; exclude_percentile 50 (at 30-55% flagged pixels the default kept only 2-24% of boxes, causing the detector-edge residual, #80); wavelengths from the frame's own WCS; no R7 gain; our defect mask. REJECTED: the free sky constant per tile, which without neighbours modelled biases single-source fluxes 6-9% low (−0.84σ). NOT ADOPTED: joint neighbour fitting (fixes crowded fluxes and gave Δσ −2.37 × 10⁻³ in the audit, but kept single-source for speed, EXT-62/EXT-16); the R7 ePSF (our extractor cannot read R7 frames, #24); OUTLIER masking (+3.0% outliers); their PSF core shift (+0.9-1.7% flux, #62).

All-sky final run: one background map per frame

Why a wavelength layer

On the filter, wavelength is a curve across the detector, and the sky has spectral lines. He I is 27 nm wide; at 48 bins per frame (the published default) each bin is 8 nm and the line's edge leaks. 288 bins follow it.

Why halo-aware

Inside the disc of a Ks < 8 star the MP_SOURCE flag is ignored for the local map, so the map sees the halo and removes it. The star's core and compact sources stay masked.

This is "tjhec": the tractorjax-spherex background (Bahk et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.19875) with two changes of ours. Code: spherex-allsky src/spectra/extract_spectra.py, tjmap_background (line 367) and frame_background; the profile sets bkg_model="tjhec" (src/run_profiles.py). Built once per frame and subtracted pixel by pixel; no ring term enters the flux. Layers: (1) ZODI. (2) B(CWAVE): the median of IMAGE − ZODI in quantile bins of per-pixel central wavelength (from the frame's own wavelength WCS on an 8 px grid), 288 bins per frame, at least 20 px per bin, interpolated with PCHIP; all MP_SOURCE pixels masked for this layer. (3) photutils Background2D on what remains: box 10 px, filter 3, 3σ clip, SExtractor estimator, exclude_percentile 50 (photutils default 10). Halo-aware mask: inside the bright-star veto disc (radius 0.1° × 10^(0.2(5.5 − Ks)), capped at 3°) of 2MASS stars with Ks < 8 (1,341,326 stars), MP_SOURCE pixels are un-masked for the Background2D step; still masked: the star core (inside 0.1 of the radius), MP_SOURCE components smaller than 1,500 px, and pixels within 2 px of a >3σ excess over a 15×15 local mean. Ks < 8 rather than 6 because the deep flagged discs outside the Ks < 6 vetoes are mostly Ks 6-8 stars (DECISIONS 2026-10-07). He I: in a whole D1 frame 48 bins are 0.008 µm wide while the 1.083 µm edge rises 20× in about 0.015 µm; that left ±0.04-0.17 MJy/sr, and the 6.5% of observations affected carried 85% of the redshift gap. 288 bins recovered 81% of the gain (results/bkgmap/port_diag.json, disc288_score.json). Figure: results/figures/fig_hei_profile.png (FWHM 27.0 nm against a pixel bandwidth of 29 nm; right panel, 300 D1 frames). The fit also masks co-add channels 14-15 (ZP-42). Large SGA galaxies are the exception: their extended-model fit (Msky) uses IMAGE − ZODI with its own free flat sky, because the 10 px boxes are about the size of a large galaxy and would remove its outskirts.

Background subtraction, step by step

Top two rows: the all-sky final run (final_v2, profile "final", background "tjhec"). Bottom lane: the September Euclid DR1 × SPHEREx catalogue. The v2 catalogue in this talk uses the top two rows unchanged (euclid-spherex src/run_profiles.py, profile "euclid": bkg_model tjhec, bright_kmax 8, fit_weights clip3, mask_outlier False, psf_zone_onebased True). Per frame, once (spherex-allsky src/spectra/extract_spectra.py, frame_background → tjmap_background): read the L2B frame's IMAGE, ZODI, FLAGS, VARIANCE and wavelength WCS; subtract the shipped zodiacal model; subtract B(λ), the median of IMAGE − ZODI in 288 quantile bins of per-pixel wavelength (≥ 20 px per bin, PCHIP interpolation, every MP_SOURCE and defect pixel masked); then photutils Background2D on the remainder (box 10 px, filter 3, 3σ clip, SExtractor estimator, exclude_percentile 50) with the halo-aware mask: inside the veto disc r(Ks) = 0.1° × 10^(0.2(5.5 − Ks)), capped at 3°, of each 2MASS Ks < 8 star, MP_SOURCE pixels are un-masked so the map follows the halo; the star core, compact sources and >3σ bumps stay masked. The sum is one background map per frame, about 3 s per frame, ~5% of extraction. Per target in each frame: a 33 × 33 px stamp; data = IMAGE − map pixel by pixel; Tractor solves only for the PSF flux at the fixed optical position (sky fixed at zero), with clip3 weights (variance minus g·clip(d, ±3σ)). Co-add: every observation of the target into 77 channels at R = 40, the same grid as the Euclid catalogue (spherex-allsky src/spectra/coadd_stream.py channel_grid). Redshift fit: eazy-py, with co-add channels 14-15 (He I) masked. Dashed, side branches: the 4-6 px ring median (clean pixels; if fewer than 4, a 3σ clipped median over the same ring with flagged pixels kept, "F4") is still computed per observation and stored as bkg_annulus, with the F4c flux from a direct weighted solve; it is a diagnostic and a fallback the release co-add can choose, not the flux. SGA large galaxies also get an extended-model fit (Msky) on IMAGE − ZODI with its own free flat sky, because the 10 px boxes are about the size of a large galaxy; stored, nothing fits redshifts from it yet. Frames that are almost entirely flagged (~1.7%) have no B(λ); the choice there is made at the release co-add. Bright stars, all-sky final run: the red box is the one place the processing differs near a star. Inside the veto disc of a 2MASS Ks < 8 star (1,341,326 stars), pixels the L2B pipeline flagged as MP_SOURCE are un-masked for the Background2D step, so the local map follows the halo and subtracts it; the star core (inside 0.1 of the radius), MP_SOURCE components smaller than 1,500 px and pixels near >3σ bumps stay masked. Outside the discs all flagged pixels are masked. The wavelength profile B(λ) masks all flagged pixels everywhere. Separately, the release carries a bright-star veto flag (bs_ratio) for selecting samples; the fiducial clustering footprint does not apply it (robustness arm only). Euclid lane (euclid-spherex src/spectra/extract_spectra.py:228-238): no frame-level map. Nothing is different near a bright star: inside its flagged disc the ring has no clean pixels, so nothing is subtracted and the halo light enters the flux. The only bright-star step is at sample level: the galaxy sample drops objects within 45″ of Gaia G < 13 stars. Per stamp: IMAGE − ZODI, minus the median of the 4-6 px ring's clean, unflagged pixels, or zero if fewer than 4 survive; Tractor PSF flux at the fixed Euclid position with 1/variance weights; co-add onto 77 channels; joint redshift fit with the Euclid and ground bands.
