Use the density field to understand intrinsic alignments

  • The density field has high signal-to-noise: millions of well-measured modes.
  • Galaxy shapes carry the same gravitational information at much lower signal-to-noise, buried under shape noise.
  • So construct templates from the density field: physical predictions for the shape field, such as linear tidal alignment (LTA) or tidal torquing (QTA).
  • Cross-correlate each template with the shapes, and learn which physics the shapes follow.

A random density field (blue, deeper where dense), its tidal field (black: the direction it stretches along), and galaxies (red) elongated along it, as linear tidal alignment predicts.

The idea of the whole IA project. The density field, here CMASS, has very high signal-to-noise: millions of well-measured modes. The galaxy shapes respond to the same gravitational field, but each shape is dominated by its intrinsic scatter.

So rather than fit a generic amplitude to the shapes, construct physical templates from the density field: the linear tidal alignment template, the quadratic tidal-torquing template, an advected version, and the velocity. Each is a prediction for the shape field. Cross-correlating it with the measured shapes tests that physics directly.

The figure: a slice around an overdensity. LTA stretches shapes radially towards the halo; QTA predicts tangential alignment; around a halo they oppose, around a void they agree.

From the IAP talk (intrinsic_alignments/documentation/presentations/iap_2026, slides 7–8); figure figures/output/decals/headline/template_slice_halo.png.

2D lensing

The paper

Murray, Kilbinger & Payerne: "Two-dimensional weak lensing shear for cluster mass, concentration, ellipticity and miscentering estimation". A&A format.

Check before quoting the abstract: it says "up to 20%" on the mass and "a factor of 2" for the line of sight; the body and conclusions say 3–18% and 2.2.

2D lensing · the motivation

Use the whole shear field, not its average in rings

\(g_+\) (left) and \(g_\times\) (right) round one simulated cluster before shape noise: \(M_{200m} = 8\times10^{14}\,M_\odot\), \(c = 4\), \(e = 0.4\), 0.2′ off-centre.

1D: 15 rings

2D: × 10 sectors

The motivation, from the paper's introduction. Using the 2D field rather than the azimuthally averaged profile has two advantages. Miscentring and ellipticity create non-circular signals that are lost to the circular average, and the 2D field measures them directly. And the cosmic web shears the galaxies too, adding noise that is correlated across the field; averaging combines it sub-optimally (Murray+ 2025).

The model: an elliptical NFW halo, the correlated structure from Quijote N-body boxes, the uncorrelated line of sight as a Gaussian field from C_ℓ, and shape noise for 10, 30 or 100 galaxies per arcmin². g+ and g× in 15 rings from 1 to 32 arcmin, each cut into 10 sectors, go into neural posterior estimation (sbi), trained on 10⁶ simulations.

Figure: paper Fig. 1, laid side by side; rings and sectors drawn for the talk (scripts/make_diagrams.py).

2D lensing · the results

The 2D field tightens every parameter

Mean posterior width over 800 noise realisations per point: 1D in red, 2D in blue; dotted, solid, dashed for 10, 30, 100 galaxies per arcmin² (paper Fig. 8b).

Mass8% tighterup to 18% at 100 per arcmin²

Gains at 30 galaxies per arcmin², Euclid-like.

At 30 galaxies per arcmin²: mass 8% tighter, concentration 11%, miscentring 21% (aanda.tex L314). At 100 per arcmin²: 18%, 19% and 32%. Ellipticity improves 6–8% at 30 and 10–13% at 100, but for a single cluster it stays prior-dominated: that is what stacks are for.

The mass gain comes from handling the correlated noise: without the line-of-sight structure it essentially disappears (L300). And leaving the line of sight out underestimates the mass error by 2.2× for the most massive clusters (L298).

SPHEREx

A spectrum of the whole sky, from space

From the ground, the near-infrared is cut into narrow windows. SPHEREx and Euclid observe it from space.
How SPHEREx scans the sky. Animation: NASA/JPL-Caltech (NASA SVS), "Novel Survey Strategy".
  • Launched 11 March 2025, surveying since 1 May 2025; a 20 cm telescope, 3.5° × 11.3° field
  • The whole sky every six months, 0.75–5 µm in 102 channels, with 6.2″ pixels
  • Polar orbit, looking about 90° from the Sun: the ecliptic poles are crossed on every orbit

Why space: from the ground, the near-infrared is chopped into narrow windows by water vapour and CO₂, and beyond about 2.5 µm the sky and the telescope glow. SPHEREx is not new because of its band; it is new because it takes a spectrum of every point on the sky. WISE did the sky in 4 broad bands; SPHEREx does it in 102 channels.

The survey: it observes near the great circle 90° from the Sun, which sweeps round the sky as the Earth goes round the Sun, so the whole sky is covered every six months. The poles are crossed on nearly every orbit, which is why the deep fields are there and why our depth depends on ecliptic latitude.

From the pizza-lunch deck (euclid-spherex/docs/slides/pizza_lunch_cea). Numbers: Bock et al. 2025 (arXiv:2511.02985). The spectrum figure's opacity curve is schematic; the facility ranges are real.

  • To learn how to do something, do it
    • You make mistakes, and improve

Why this was the right project to push the agentic approach on. It was outside my expertise, so I could not carry it on my own knowledge. It produces something concrete. And the quality is easy to see: a bad catalogue fails against spectroscopic redshifts immediately, so there is nowhere for plausible-but-wrong to hide.

The wall: an analysis as blocks, each with its own inputs and outputs and a check around it, stacked one course on the last.

But blocks are not bricks. The wall settles to a ghost while blocks lift off it: they overlap; large blocks contain smaller ones; and a small block can contain a large one, one stage of a pipeline being itself a whole wall at the level below. It plays once and stays there.

And they do not stay put. Blocks are made, grow, shrink, collapse, crumble, and disappear; it never plays the same way twice.

Blocks

Blocks: try to build slowly, not just A->Z

  • What it is
    • Its own inputs and outputs, and a check around it that must pass against something outside itself before anything is built on top
  • Its four parts
    • Interface: inputs and outputs, so blocks compose and many can be built in parallel
    • Contract: a check outside it; when it fails, the agent revisits its assumptions
    • Decisions: its own choices, explicit, implicit and inherited, logged in ASTRA
    • Size: a variable; how big a block can be is the open question
  • Two ceilings on block size
    • Agent reliability: how much an agent can do and get right. Evals measure it, and new models move it
    • Human understanding: evals cannot move it, and that is the point. The analysis cannot advance faster than I understand it

A block is the smallest unit of an analysis that has its own inputs and outputs, can be handed to an agent whole, and has a check around it that must pass against something outside itself before anything is built on top (agentic-science/blocks/README.md).

The size of a block is the variable, and it has two ceilings. One is the agent's reliability, which evals measure and new models move. The other is my understanding, which evals cannot move.

Sky cells on a fixed 10° lattice (src/spectra/campaign.py), 358 cells shared between the nibi and fir clusters by a hash-chained ledger. Frame index: one IRSA TAP query on spherex.artifact × spherex.plane, QR2 only, pinned by sha1 (src/spectra/build_frame_index.py); per cell, a frame is kept if any target lies inside its footprint (17 px margin), no frame cap (cell_index.py, extract_frames.py). Staging: from the S3 Open Data mirror (HTTPS fallback to IRSA) onto node-local disk in 2,000-frame batches, the next staging while the current is measured, each deleted once measured (docs/STORAGE.md).

Per frame: each frame is opened once (frame-major), IMAGE, VARIANCE, FLAGS, ZODI, PSF cube and the wavelength WCS read (src/spectra/extract_spectra.py ~785-819); the background map (tjhec) and the variance gain for the clipped weights are made per frame. Per target on that frame: 33 × 33 px stamp; the PSF of the nearest PSF-cube zone, 10× oversampled, spline-shifted to the sub-pixel offset and summed to 9 × 9 px; defect mask (OUTLIER unmasked in the final run); Tractor PointSource with the position frozen and the sky fixed at zero, fitted to IMAGE − map with clip3 weights. Output per observation: flux, error, aperture flux, λ, Δλ from the wavelength WCS, flags, the ring diagnostic and the background subtracted; F4c flux by a direct solve; Msky for SGA galaxies.

Per target: the archive keeps every observation (about 3.7 × 10¹⁰ rows, ~1.74 TB; OPERATOR_BRIEF), so the co-add can be rebuilt without re-extracting. Co-add: 77 channels at R = 40 from 0.75 to 5.0 µm (coadd_stream.py channel_grid), each error inflated by the frozen per-detector factor (1.03-1.28); the stream co-add has no clip; the release co-add rebuilt from the archive adds the l3 gain, rejection and the all-source-frame veto. Zero points measured on DESI (1.08-1.11 over 3.8-5 µm) but not applied in prelim_v0. Redshift: eazy-py, fsps_QSF_12_v3, flat prior, empirical error model, He I channels 14-15 masked, log grid from z = 0.001, P(z) stored; SPHEREx channels only. Stellar fit: eazy's PHOENIX/Sonora grid, is_star when χ²_star − χ²_gal < 0. Quality bits stored raw; samples defined at release.

Euclid (docs/reports/final_2026-09-28 §3): 13,784,773 VIS-detected targets; 212 cells of 4° with a 6,000-frame cap (31 cells hit it, ~21% of targets); the earlier extractor (ring background, OUTLIER masked, 1/VARIANCE weights); a batch co-add onto the same 77 channels with a 3σ clip; zero points fitted at the spec-z on half the truth, iterated, frozen, applied to the Euclid and ground bands with SPHEREx as the anchor; a joint fit of SPHEREx + Euclid VIS/Y/J/H + ground bands.

SPHEREx all-sky · the background

Background subtraction, step by step

Top two rows: the all-sky final run (final_v2, profile "final", background "tjhec"). Bottom lane: the September Euclid DR1 × SPHEREx catalogue. The v2 catalogue in this talk uses the top two rows unchanged (euclid-spherex src/run_profiles.py, profile "euclid": bkg_model tjhec, bright_kmax 8, fit_weights clip3, mask_outlier False, psf_zone_onebased True).

Per frame, once (spherex-allsky src/spectra/extract_spectra.py, frame_background → tjmap_background): read the L2B frame's IMAGE, ZODI, FLAGS, VARIANCE and wavelength WCS; subtract the shipped zodiacal model; subtract B(λ), the median of IMAGE − ZODI in 288 quantile bins of per-pixel wavelength (≥ 20 px per bin, PCHIP interpolation, every MP_SOURCE and defect pixel masked); then photutils Background2D on the remainder (box 10 px, filter 3, 3σ clip, SExtractor estimator, exclude_percentile 50) with the halo-aware mask: inside the veto disc r(Ks) = 0.1° × 10^(0.2(5.5 − Ks)), capped at 3°, of each 2MASS Ks < 8 star, MP_SOURCE pixels are un-masked so the map follows the halo; the star core, compact sources and >3σ bumps stay masked. The sum is one background map per frame, about 3 s per frame, ~5% of extraction.

Per target in each frame: a 33 × 33 px stamp; data = IMAGE − map pixel by pixel; Tractor solves only for the PSF flux at the fixed optical position (sky fixed at zero), with clip3 weights (variance minus g·clip(d, ±3σ)). Co-add: every observation of the target into 77 channels at R = 40, the same grid as the Euclid catalogue (spherex-allsky src/spectra/coadd_stream.py channel_grid). Redshift fit: eazy-py, with co-add channels 14-15 (He I) masked.

Dashed, side branches: the 4-6 px ring median (clean pixels; if fewer than 4, a 3σ clipped median over the same ring with flagged pixels kept, "F4") is still computed per observation and stored as bkg_annulus, with the F4c flux from a direct weighted solve; it is a diagnostic and a fallback the release co-add can choose, not the flux. SGA large galaxies also get an extended-model fit (Msky) on IMAGE − ZODI with its own free flat sky, because the 10 px boxes are about the size of a large galaxy; stored, nothing fits redshifts from it yet. Frames that are almost entirely flagged (~1.7%) have no B(λ); the choice there is made at the release co-add.

Bright stars, all-sky final run: the red box is the one place the processing differs near a star. Inside the veto disc of a 2MASS Ks < 8 star (1,341,326 stars), pixels the L2B pipeline flagged as MP_SOURCE are un-masked for the Background2D step, so the local map follows the halo and subtracts it; the star core (inside 0.1 of the radius), MP_SOURCE components smaller than 1,500 px and pixels near >3σ bumps stay masked. Outside the discs all flagged pixels are masked. The wavelength profile B(λ) masks all flagged pixels everywhere. Separately, the release carries a bright-star veto flag (bs_ratio) for selecting samples; the fiducial clustering footprint does not apply it (robustness arm only).

Euclid lane (euclid-spherex src/spectra/extract_spectra.py:228-238): no frame-level map. Nothing is different near a bright star: inside its flagged disc the ring has no clean pixels, so nothing is subtracted and the halo light enters the flux. The only bright-star step is at sample level: the galaxy sample drops objects within 45″ of Gaia G < 13 stars. Per stamp: IMAGE − ZODI, minus the median of the 4-6 px ring's clean, unflagged pixels, or zero if fewer than 4 survive; Tractor PSF flux at the fixed Euclid position with 1/variance weights; co-add onto 77 channels; joint redshift fit with the Euclid and ground bands.

[click] An orchestrator, the main Claude session, asks sub-agents to work on them, three or four at a time. Each works on one issue in its own git worktree, on its own branch, and posts its results on the issue; the orchestrator merges.

SPHEREx all-sky · solved along the way

We can see airglow from helium with SPHEREx

The low Earth orbit means that SPHEREx sees some of the atmosphere.
The Sun lights up He I in the upper atmosphere, and SPHEREx sees it as emission at 1.083 µm.
Read naively, it looks like Hα.

Metastable 2³S helium in the upper atmosphere resonantly scatters sunlight in the 2³S–2³P transition at 1.083 µm; Hubble's WFC3 sees it as a time-varying background (Brammer et al. 2014).

In one SPHEREx D1 frame (2025-10-20) the sky is 0.40 MJy/sr at 1.040 µm, 2.43 on the line and 0.47 at 1.106 µm. SPHEREx has a linear variable filter, so the orange curve is where the filter passes 1.083 µm. In the co-added spectra a residual on SPHEREx channels 14–15 is read as Hα at z ≈ 0.67, which made the alias pile at 0.6–0.68 and the cliff at 0.68 in the catalogue (issue #11).

The fix so far is a fit-level mask of the He I channels; the next step is SPHEREx's published He I background template (#49), and the adopted tractorjax-spherex background with 288 wavelength bins catches the line's edge across a whole frame (DECISIONS, EXT-20). In the Euclid v2 fit the He I channels are masked and the z ≈ 0.67 band is gone.

Euclid × SPHEREx · the clustering

Galaxy correlations

  • Randoms generated within the footprint
  • Iterative systematics weights, as in DES
  • Maps: dust, star density, SPHEREx depth, ecliptic latitude, zodiacal background, VIS depth, …
  • Cross-correlations between distant shells close to zero: no more anti-correlation round bright stars

True n(z) of six Euclid × SPHEREx bins from reweighted spectra, against the DES Y6 lens bins (arXiv:2601.14559); each scaled to peak 1.

Resolved Euclid galaxy sample in six shells from 0.05 to 0.6. Landy–Szalay cross-correlations against one shared random set (11.2 M randoms, 10× the largest shell), each galaxy weighted by its own shell's ISD weight, 120 jackknife patches. N per shell: 232k, 790k, 1.09 M, 1.12 M, 716k, 429k.

r over 10′–60′, weighted: neighbouring shells 0.15–0.33 (the overlap of their true n(z), as expected for σ_NMAD ≈ 0.009 and Δz = 0.1 shells); two or more apart −0.03 to 0.08. In September and in the first all-sky run, bright-star halos made far shells anti-correlate (r = −0.58 for 0.1–0.2 × 0.6–0.7); no longer.

Weights: ISD as in DES Y3/Y6, eight survey-property maps, five regressed (E(B−V), stars, SPHEREx channel count, ecliptic latitude, VIS depth); the most significant map is corrected and the loop repeats until every S_1D < 2, with significance from 400 lognormal mocks. Weights span 0.77–1.36.

A story about agents on someone else's machine. I was running jobs on candide from nibi, and every agent had to open its own SSH connection. Then one agent tried to connect 200 times in 8 seconds; candide's port knock read that as an attack, and we were locked out.

The orchestrator then worked out that agents could be sent through the connection another agent already had open. The agent working on issue #73 came along and decided that was not allowed.

Afterwards we went through candide's rules together, and they now go into the brief: the shared connection only, with no retry loops; the cusers account on comp, no GPUs, never node n09; small writes under /n09data/murray; rsync the code over and bring results back to nibi to commit; never read another user's data.

The repo's handoff says the same: one shared SSH master, never open new connections in loops (docs/HANDOFF.md).

From Euclid to the entire extragalactic sky

The Euclid DR1 × SPHEREx galaxies glide onto the whole sky; the all-sky SPHEREx catalogue (Legacy Surveys DR9, DR10 and DR11 positions) fills in around them, and Euclid's footprint stays in orange.

1. The two Euclid DR1 lobes, one shell, 0.07 < z < 0.10 (58,643 galaxies, v2 SPHEREx-only resolved galaxy sample). 2. Every galaxy glides to its place on an equatorial Mollweide: the north lobe ends as slivers near the pole, the south lobe lands in the DES part of the southern sky. 3. The all-sky SPHEREx catalogue fades in around them, same shell (1,096,235 galaxies): SPHEREx forced photometry at Legacy Surveys DR9-north + DR10-south positions and, where those have no grz, the DR11 rerun, which replaces DELVE. Same method, different parent catalogue, redshifts from SPHEREx alone. 4. Dust in our universe · magnification

Quasars behind SPHEREx galaxies are magnified

Quasars from three surveys, stacked behind the all-sky SPHEREx galaxies: the closer the galaxy, the brighter the quasar, in every band. Magnification is the same in every band; dust dims blue bands more than red ones, so the colours of the same shifts measure the dust.

Using:

The dust_in_our_universe project: background quasars from Quaia, DESI DR1 and SDSS DR16Q stacked behind LSx galaxies (the all-sky SPHEREx catalogue), in physical r_p per pair. Magnification is achromatic and extinction is not, so the chromaticity of the quasar magnitude shifts separates the two; W1 and W2 anchor the magnification level.

Joint W1+W2 GLS fit of A (r_p / 1 Mpc)^−0.8 above 100 kpc, jackknife covariance with the Hartlap factor: S/N 9.2 (Quaia, 601,446 quasars), 11.1 (DESI, 1,126,816), 5.3 (SDSS, 681,936). Below 100 kpc the shifts are set by the photometry of a quasar beside a neighbour, which differs between surveys.

Universe mass_weighted (lenses weighted by stellar mass; the baseline gives S/N 8.0, 4.3, 1.1). The LSx build it uses is prelim_v0_dr11d, marked preliminary and not for science: say so if numbers are asked for.
